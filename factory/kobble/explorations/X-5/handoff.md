# Send feedback from anywhere, use it in the factory

**The problem:** Every team running a factory finds bugs and wishes, but has no way to send them to the people who make the factory, and the factory's own errors are lost. We want a general way to send from any factory, with nothing to host, and one place in our factory that uses it.

**The question:** How does an app send to the factory?

**Agreed:** E · A public feedback board. A separate public GitHub repository just for feedback; anyone with a GitHub account sends from their factory, your code stays private, nothing to host.

**Variant:** F · Tell the kobold. No form. In the "Ask Sprocket anything" bar the person just says or types the wish or bug in their own words ("I wish I could…"). The kobold asks one or two short questions, finds similar posts on the public board ("3 teams want this too — add your 👍?"), writes a clean title and description, and shows the draft as a card with what goes along (version, page, errors with any team data highlighted to remove). One "Send" with their GitHub sign-in; afterwards it follows the post's state (sent, seen, planned, done) in the same chat. Errors still pop up as "Report this error", which opens this chat already filled in.

The prototype holds the variants E, F, shown by the page address (`?variant=F`). Build only F: leave out the others and the variant switch.

**Changed along the way** (all of it is on the prototype's branch, and all of it is to be built):
1. Show the sending side, inside any factory: a "Send feedback" button always at hand (sidebar). It opens a short form: wish or bug, a title, a few words. When the factory hits an error, it offers "Report this error" with the details filled in. Before sending, the person sees exactly what will go out and can edit or remove any part (errors can carry their team's data). Sending uses their own GitHub sign-in and lands on a public feedback board on GitHub; afterwards they see a link to it there and its state later (seen, planned, done).
2. Variant F ("Tell the kobold") only; leave variant E as it is. F is too minimal: people don't know what to do. "Send feedback" in the sidebar (and "Report this error") opens a small, clean support window (a panel docked at the bottom right, not the tiny bar) with a header "Help & feedback". Before any chat, it shows three clear starting choices as tappable rows: "Something is wrong", "I wish Kobble could…", "Ask a question", plus a small "Your posts (2)" link showing their sent posts and states. Picking one starts the chat with Sprocket in that window with a matching first line and two or three example quick replies; the rest stays as it is (short questions, similar posts with 👍, draft card with what goes along and team data to remove, Send with GitHub sign-in, following the state). Keep it minimal: few words, lots of space, no form fields.
3. Variant F only; leave E as it is. Merge the two: no separate "Help & feedback" panel. Everything opens in the one Sprocket window: clicking "Ask Sprocket anything", "Send feedback" or "Report this error" makes the bar grow smoothly into the Sprocket window. Make that window feel alive rather than a static chat: (1) its opening adapts to where you are, e.g. on the Factory page "Builder stopped? Report it", plus the three starting choices (Something is wrong, I wish Kobble could…, Ask a question) as small chips, and a waiting error shows as the first suggestion; (2) while you talk, the post builds itself live in a card pinned at the top of the window (title, what happened, what goes along fill in and update as you answer), so you see the result taking shape instead of a wall of messages; (3) Sprocket's replies type in, with a short "thinking" moment, and quick replies change with each step; (4) similar posts slide in as you type, with 👍; (5) after Send the card turns into a small live status (sent → seen → planned → done) and "Your posts" is a tab in the window header. Keep it minimal and calm: few words, soft motion, no form fields.

**The prototype:** on the branch `proto/X-5-e`.

**What we learned:**
- Errors can carry a team's own data. Before anything is sent, the person sees exactly what goes out and can edit or remove it.
- Before it goes live: the team creates the public feedback board on GitHub; the new words get Italian and German; Sprocket gets real thinking instead of the prototype's script.
